Shared month-first parser

The shared parser reads dates month first. Day-first tenant inputs can therefore exchange day and month. Apply the tenant directory and channel, not the reporter’s browser locale or intake guess. A booking-v3 tenant’s app or email report still follows this route.

For example, R-41205 says: “Customer booked 3 October and got an invite for 10 March. Please look at this.” Its browser is en-US and intake guessed hint; its tenant morrow.tax is fr-FR on booking-v2. Those intake fields do not establish the cause.

Fix direction: preserve the tenant’s declared date semantics through shared parsing, or use an explicit unambiguous representation between systems. Verify dates with both fields at most twelve and dates with a day above twelve, through app, email and booking-v2. The export describes the mechanism; it does not include production parser code or a verified production fix.

booking-v3 hint and parsing disagree

The form prints MM/DD/YYYY while parsing in the day-first tenant’s format. Following the hint gives the wrong date. Only form-channel reports on booking-v3 match this mechanism. Align the hint, validation and parsing with the same tenant locale. Test form submissions separately from the shared parser path.

R-41241, alder.physio on booking-v3, reports: “We had two people show up on 8 June. They were booked for 6 August.” Its form channel and tenant configuration, together with causes.ts, distinguish the route. A matching subject would not.

Calendar dates come from each original timestamp, as in QueueTimeline. A merge is an administrative event, not a fix or resolution.

The distinction between receivedAt and mergedAt matters: delayed bulk merging can conceal a long-running report stream. Use receipt dates to track occurrence reports and merge dates to audit desk processing. Neither field is the date of the reported booking event. The original prose’s “Since April” is incomplete without checking this rendered history.

Triage from now on

  1. Deduplicate export rows by report ID. Keep provenance and do not count page overlap as new demand.
  2. Read the body. Look up tenant locale and form version, and record the channel, intended date and observed date. Treat subject, browser locale and intakeTag as search clues.
  3. Merge only when evidence supports the same mechanism. Different subjects may describe one cause; identical subjects may hide different causes.
  4. Hold unknown tenants and unexplained symptoms for investigation. A classifier that cannot see a symptom cannot disprove it. Do not default to merge when uncertain.
  5. Assign an owner and a next review to every unresolved group. Recheck new reports and update counts from data; a queue marker is not proof of a fix.
  6. Record the basis of each closure: independent verification, an attributed engineering fix, or retirement of the affected feature. Verify a fix against its reports before claiming independent resolution. Keep unexplained cases visible and update the linked issues when evidence changes.
